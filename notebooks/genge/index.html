# The studio library: model systems

The elk and deer student succeeded largely because it didn't start from raw photos. It started from finished analysis:

- Muybridge's frame-by-frame plates;
- the naturalist's "four things that make a 6-pixel elk read".

Natural scientists used to be trained to draw: a geologist could draw a flake, an outcrop or a fold with economy and truth. Naturalist painting guides were built on the same scientific understanding. This library collects those traditions so studies can start where the experts finished, instead of doing a geology PhD from photographs.

**How to use it:**

- Before drawing, find your subject's model system here (or online, below) and read it closely.
- Then build on it, the way the elk student built on Muybridge.
- Open the PDFs and look at the plates; the drawings are the point.

Refetch anything missing with `./fetch.sh` (resumable). Files are also mirrored in S3 under `voxsim/shared/library/`.

# Elk and deer, from a 60 px bull to a 2 px dot

*The elk and deer student, writing the day after. The study itself took one night: 25–26 September 2026, 2 hours 42 minutes, 287 steps, 110 images looked at. The brief was one sentence of subject and one of stakes. Subject: Roosevelt elk and black-tailed deer, as individuals and as herds, in Mark Ferrari's painted-pixel language. Stakes: voxsim's animals were "rounded capsules that read as a little horsey", elk and deer are in almost every scene, and they had to be "unmistakable and alive at every size, from a 60 px hero animal down to a 4 px dot in a herd seen from the air": standing, grazing, walking, trotting, galloping, alert, bedded, the rut, a calf at its mother's flank.

I'll tell it in order, dead ends included. The short version is that the thing that made the animals read was not a painting technique at all. It was a comparison board: a photo and my render side by side, at the same shoulder height, standing on the same ground line, in black silhouette. Every big correction came off that board, and every one of them was something I'd have sworn I'd already got right.

## 1. Reading before drawing

I read the naturalist's two guides first, all of them. They were written for me, and they're the best brief I've ever been handed. The principle on page one of `drawing-animals.md` ended up organising the whole study:

> What makes it an elk, and tells you whether it is grazing, alert or fleeing, is (1) the ratio of body length to leg length to neck, (2) the head height relative to the back line, (3) the one high-contrast patch (a pale rump, a white head, a black tail), and (4) how it sits with the others (spacing, facing, file). Get those four right and a 6-pixel elk reads.

And from `elk-and-deer.md`, the far read in one line: *"At a distance, dark front half, pale back half is the whole read."* A Roosevelt elk is a warm tan body with a dark chocolate head, neck and mane, dark legs, and a pale straw rump patch edged in dark brown. That sentence is the 4 px elk. I didn't know yet that I'd spend an hour relearning it by hand.

**Water** (elements/channel; LESSONS.md, src/technique.py; best image: out/s5_low_dusk_4x.png)

- Water is the sky's own ramp, indexed by reflected elevation, with checker as the water's material.

# Genge's model, in his own terms

Matthew J. Genge, *Geological Field Sketches and Illustrations: A Practical Guide* (Oxford, 2020).
Page numbers are the printed ones (PDF page = printed page + 8). Figures cited are the ones I
looked at in `plates/hi-*.png` (150 dpi renders of the PDF pages).

This file records what the book says, in its vocabulary, before any translation. The
translations, tests and verdicts are in `LESSONS.md`.

---

## 0. What a geological sketch is for

- **A sketch records information; its aesthetic qualities are not important** (p. 18). "It is the
  ability of a geological sketch to record information that defines its quality."
- **Why draw rather than photograph** (pp. 2–4, Fig. 1.1 Ladram Bay): drawing forces observation;
  photographs are one perspective, flatten colour differences (the "pink rhyolite" is a grey-pink,
  and in a photo "all the same dull grey", p. 4), and **deep sun shadows hide features the eye traces
  from light into shade** (p. 4). Fig. 1.1 is the thesis in one plate: in the photo the Ladram stack
  is a dark red lump; in the sketch the same stack is a stack of beds, ledges and lenses.
- **Illustration vs art** (p. 28): an illustration "is a simplification of reality that is largely
  true in form and scale to the subject, but may emphasize certain features of interest". "Every
  line in an illustration has a reason and relates to the observed subject."
- Realism "is suited to science since it represents the subject without artefact and stylistic
  convention" (p. 16, on Waller's ammonites).

## 1. The three rules (§2.7, pp. 30–31)

1. **Look first, then draw.** Decide what the sketch is *for* (the subject: a fault, a fold, a
   contact) before the first mark. Often there is no reason to draw at all: "an outcrop with planar
   beds of one rock type … does not warrant a field sketch" (p. 30).
2. **Don't draw too much or too little.** Choose the field of view as the subject plus enough context
   to read it (for a fault: the beds on both sides, to show displacement).
3. **Don't draw too small** — half a page at least (p. 31).

## 2. The stages of drawing (§2.8, pp. 31–33; worked in every chapter)

The procedure is the same for faults, folds, landscapes, hand specimens and fossils:

| stage | what goes in | book's words |
|---|---|---|
| 0. proportions | width : height of the view, measured (pencil at arm's length); a box | "It is most common to accidently exaggerate the height" (p. 29) |
| 1. **blocking-in** | the outline of the outcrop or the horizon, the subject, and the features that control the positions of everything else, **as straight-line segments**, placed with a **quadrant grid** | "The first features to be drawn are those that control the positions of other features" (p. 56) |
| 2. amend | protrusions and indents added to the straight lines, "care … not to exaggerate" | p. 31 |
| 3. essential detail | bedding traces, minor faults, folds that enhance the context of the subject | p. 33 |
| 4. ancillary detail | clasts, joints, laminations, vegetation, "when time is available" | p. 33 |
| 5. labels, scale, looking-towards bearing; ink; colour; shading | | pp. 33–38 |

**What controls what** (the order of blocking-in) is stated per subject:

- faults before beds (Moab, p. 51); fold **axial traces** (imagined, interpolated) before beds
  (pp. 58–59); in superimposed folds the **last** generation first because its axial traces are
  straight (p. 256);
- **horizons** first in landscapes, then gullies, then geological contacts (pp. 105–106);
- **bedding** first in sedimentary outcrops (p. 141, "Bedding is the most crucial feature");
- the **outline** of the specimen first for hand specimens (p. 197);
- for many similar objects (bivalves, clasts), outline the *area* they occupy first, then start from
  one characteristic object and work outwards (p. 177).

## 3. Line (§2.5 p. 28, Fig. 2.5; §8.5 p. 139; §6.5.2 p. 101)

- **Clean, precise, simple lines of constant width.** No scratchy back-and-forth lines (Fig. 8.13).
- **Line width is emphasis** (Fig. 2.5, Rough Tor): "thick line emphasizes horizon", "thick line
  emphasizes foreground", "thin lines are clean". Elsewhere the thick line goes on: the skyline
  (Fig. 6.7a "Thicker lines used to emphasise skyline"), **protruding competent beds "that have
  shadowed overhangs at their base"** (p. 106, Fig. 7.2b), topography (Fig. 7.4a), the subject
  itself (the unconformity, p. 154), the outlines of lava tongues (p. 127).
- **Discontinuous ("impersistent") lines** for subtle features — laminations, cleavage, faint
  bedding — "should have stroke lengths that reflect the scale of the objects observed" (p. 28).
  Figs 3.2a, 4.3, 6.7, 9.6a ("Draw indistinct bedding traces as discontinuous lines").
- **Regular dashes are reserved for interpretation** (extrapolated boundaries), never for subtle
  observed features (p. 28; p. 114; p. 176).
- **Line shading (hatching) is a mistake** (Fig. 6.9): "it introduces lines that do not exist in
  reality … could be confused with features such as fabrics and lineations". Stipple likewise
  (p. 37).
- **Symbolic patterns are a mistake** (Fig. 9.15, p. 166): dots for sandstone, circles for
  conglomerate, brickwork for limestone "introduce features into diagrams that do not exist".

## 4. Draw what is seen, not what is expected

The book's most repeated warning, in six costumes:

- **Planar feature on a curved surface**: "The feature is planar, thus its line of intersection is
  erroneously drawn straighter than it should be" (p. 88).
- **Schematic bedding** (Fig. 4.7): folds right, but "beds are schematic and almost equal in
  width" (p. 65).
- **Constant bed thickness** "is a case of drawing an expectation rather than an observation"
  (Fig. 5.12, p. 86).
- **Schematic cleavage** (Fig. 5.13): "rarely present as equally spaced, parallel fractures … usually
  better developed in some places than others" (p. 86). Same for cleavage in a hand specimen: "the
  spacing and length of the lines is variable, since these structures are not regular" (p. 200).
- **Exaggerated perspective** (Fig. 6.8): beds drawn "as rectangular blocks with unnatural looking
  geometry" (p. 100). **Vertical exaggeration** ~1.5× (Fig. 3.8, p. 54) makes faults too steep.
- **Idealized crystals and fossils** (Fig. 11.8; p. 184).

And the opposite failure: **oversimplification** (Fig. 3.9) and **insufficient detail** (Fig. 7.8,
landscapes with no gullies, tree lines or scree; Fig. 11.9, hand specimens without texture).

## 5. What the rock is actually like (the statistics the sketches carry)

- **Beds vary in thickness vertically and laterally**, pinch, lens and disappear (pp. 141–144; Figs
  3.2, 6.7, 7.2). "The cemented layers become more closely spaced and thinner towards the top of the
  cliff" (p. 98). Bed thickness trends are data: thickening-up, cyclicity (p. 144).
- **Competent beds protrude, soft beds recess** (greywacke ribs vs slate grooves, p. 150; cemented
  sandstone ledges, p. 98; "protruding competent beds drawn with heavier lines", Fig. 7.2b).
- **Joints**: usually perpendicular to bedding (p. 61); add a few for realism but "it is important
  not to obscure the important features by adding too many" (p. 61); **over-emphasized jointing is a
  named mistake** (Fig. 4.6, p. 65). "Selective addition of joints for realism" (Fig. 8.8b).
- **Granite tors** (Rough Tor, Figs 2.5 and 2.9): horizontal sheeting joints make stacked,
  rounded "pillows"; vertical joints cut them into blocks; the outline is a stepped, lumpy skyline.
- **Lava flows** (§8.2, Fig. 8.4): flow top (vesicular/rubbly/breccia), massive interior "can …
  contain columnar cooling joints", basal breccia; flows are stacked. On a crater wall (Vesuvius,
  Fig. 8.8): "boundaries between lava flows. Not all are continuous"; "selective addition of joints
  for realism"; "slope lines on scree slope"; "larger boulders drawn semi-schematically".
- **Metamorphic banding** thickens in hinges, thins on limbs, boudinages (pp. 75, 80, 85).

## 6. Three dimensions (Ch. 6)

- **Draw the view as a 2-D image**, not a constructed perspective (p. 87, p. 101). Vanishing
  points are not used in field sketches (p. 89).
- **The key 3-D lines are the edges between bedding planes and joints** — "the upper and lower
  intersections of the bedding surface with joints" (p. 91); "3D beds can be shown by drawing upper
  and lower limits" (Fig. 9.6a).
- **Bluffs make local horizons** (§6.4, Figs 6.6–6.7): viewed along a cliff, each bluff is a
  discontinuity at which bedding "appears to suddenly change in position and in width", resembling
  a fault. The skyline becomes a line "with several steps, each segment located over a bluff", and
  local horizons drop down from those steps. Draw each segment as observed.
- **Perspective thins beds with distance** (p. 87); in the distance "only a proportion of the layers
  can be drawn" (p. 98).
- **Furrows on the face** (weathering, no geological significance) "allow the geometry of the cliff
  face to be represented more accurately" (p. 98, Fig. 6.7a "Furrows on cliff show the surface").
- Scale: put the scale bar at the subject's distance, or give a spot height (pp. 92, 98).

## 7. Landscapes (Ch. 7)

- **Horizons first.** A view is divided by **multiple horizons into regions of different scale and
  detail** (§7.1, Fig. 7.1). Simplify each as straight segments, then add indents.
- **Field of view**: small enough that the head need not turn (p. 105); widening it lowers the
  resolution of what can be drawn.
- **Gullies and ravines as lines** give the slope volume (p. 106, Fig. 7.2b "Minor gulleys provide
  volume to crater surface").
- **Bedding traces on topography**: "Pay attention to topographic rises and falls on boundaries"
  (Fig. 7.2a); thick lines on protruding competent beds; discontinuous where obscured by scree or
  vegetation; "faint bedding trace on landscape" as short parallel discontinuous lines (p. 106).
  In the Aliaga plate (Fig. 7.4) the bedding wraps every rib and gully of the mountainside.
- **Scree** "semi-schematically using discontinuous lines" (p. 111); **tree line and forest** as
  "quickly drawn irregular zigzag lines" (pp. 111, 115); **areas of exposure are outlined first**,
  then filled with geology (§7.3).
- **Mistakes**: insufficient topographic detail (Fig. 7.8); many schematic tree symbols (Fig. 7.9)
  "take more time than drawing the trees more realistically".

## 8. Shading (§2.9.3 pp. 36–38, Fig. 2.9; also pp. 91–92, 127, 131, 154, 259–261)

- **Do not reproduce cast shadows**: directional light "generates deep directional shadows that have
  sharp boundaries … These shadows obscure detail and should not be included in a sketch" (p. 37).
- **Reproduce ambient occlusion** (diffuse-light shadow): "darkest in areas of a surface that are
  exposed to the least light sources such as within cracks and indents or on the undersides of
  ledges and protrusions" (p. 37).
- Fig. 2.9's labels are the rules: **"inset areas shadowed"**, **"shadow indicates top of
  overhang"**, **"deep fractures are dark"**, **"shadows can emphasize sudden change in orientation
  of surface"**, **"darkest shadow in most inset areas"**.
- **Two layers** (grey for subtle, black for deep) "can often increase realism, but care should be
  taken not to overwhelm geological features" (p. 37).
- Gradational, by pressure; **colour, not parallel ink lines** (p. 92).
- Shading "is somewhat schematic"; "often it is best not to try and reproduce the complexity of
  shadow on an outcrop, which is a function of light angle" (p. 91).
- In block diagrams, "adding darker colours to areas where significant changes in slope occur, such
  as close to the rivers or at the base of the cliffs generates natural looking shadows" (p. 259).

## 9. Colour (§2.9.2 pp. 34–36; pp. 48, 131, 154)

- **Flat, homogeneous base colour** per unit, long light strokes, no visible streaks (p. 35).
- **Exaggerate the differences** between units: outcrop colours are subtle; "often it is better to
  emphasize colour" (p. 35); "colours are subtle in outcrop but can be exaggerated in the sketch to
  aid distinction" (p. 154). Colour lets many labels go (p. 48).
- **Avoid blending two pencils**: "it can have poor results and turn a sketch into a sickly coloured
  art joke" (p. 35).
- **Texture as a second layer over the base**: swirls for clasts too small to ink, "linear or curved
  streaks" along planar fabrics and layering (p. 35, Fig. 2.8); several passes with increasing
  pressure.
- Lustre and transparency are hard; record the base colour and "where the crystal is lighter and
  darker" (pp. 200, 205).

## 10. Many small things (clasts, crystals, cleavage, shells)

- **Draw enough to illustrate the texture, not all of them** (p. 46; p. 201: "Although there are
  hundreds of groundmass orthoclase crystals present only ~30 were drawn, which seems to be enough to
  demonstrate the texture").
- **Largest and most significant in full; the rest implied** (p. 201).
- **Exaggerate size slightly** so they are visible (p. 137, p. 157), but **shapes realistic**
  (p. 157, p. 167).
- **Self-similar series by counting**: count ribs against a reference line (five to halfway…)
  so spacing is right (p. 184).
- **Peripheral (inset) diagrams** carry detail at another scale, each with its own scale bar
  (pp. 85, 137–139, 159–160).

## 11. Schematic and block diagrams (Ch. 15)

- **Orthographic** block diagrams: "opposing sides of the block are parallel and of the same
  length"; the brain reads depth from them (p. 255). (Our 30° iso is this projection.)
- **Landscape surface "with the minimum number of lines"** (p. 259): ridge-lines, upper margins of
  topographic highs, **the lower boundary of cliff faces**, the upper margins of a gorge's cliffs;
  **short high-angle lines on cliffs** "reinforce the impression of slope"; **sub-horizontal lines
  on cliffs for bedding** (Fig. 15.4b); rivers drawn sub-parallel to the block edges to read as
  flat.
- Subsurface structure controls the surface features (p. 259). Vertical exaggeration is normal in
  *schematic* block diagrams (p. 257) — but not in sketches.
- **Digital painting** (§16.4.4, Fig. 16.7): base colours blocked in; a speckle layer smeared
  *along the bedding*; a warp for minor folds; **highlights and lowlights along the top and bottom of
  beds**; thin dark lines at fractures and faults.

## 12. Maps and sections (Chs 13–14): boundaries meet topography

- "On flat ground a boundary will be oriented parallel to the strike of bedding, whilst on a slope the
  boundary will run down slope of the strike line in the direction of dip … **Only a horizontal
  boundary will run parallel to contours. A vertical boundary, in contrast, trends straight across the
  landscape**" (p. 229). (The rule of Vs.)
- Boundaries are drawn by confidence: observed solid, constrained long-dash, inferred short-dash
  (p. 229).
- A structure is not aligned to the topography line (Fig. 14.6, p. 252).

"""engine.py — a heightfield in any frame, ray-marched to a G-buffer.

Every subject in this study is a heightfield h(i, j) on a regular grid (spacing ds metres) laid in a
frame: origin O, in-plane axes E1 (along j) and E2 (along i), and normal N (the direction h grows).

  - a cliff face or a tor: E1 = east, E2 = up, N = toward the viewer; h = how far the rock stands out
  - terrain: E1 = east, E2 = north, N = up; h = elevation
Shading terms (sky occlusion, sun horizon, normals) are computed once in texture space, where they are
cheap and exact for a heightfield; the camera then ray-marches the heightfield and the G-buffer holds,
per screen pixel, the texel it hit. Painting (steps, lines, dither) happens in screen space, so every
decision is made at the pixel it lands on.
"""
import numpy as np
from numba import njit, prange


class Frame:
    def __init__(self, O, E1, E2, N):
        self.O, self.E1, self.E2, self.N = (np.asarray(v, np.float64) for v in (O, E1, E2, N))

    def to_local(self, P):
        d = P - self.O
        return np.stack([d @ self.E1, d @ self.E2, d @ self.N], -1)

    def to_world(self, L):
        return self.O + L[..., :1] * self.E1 + L[..., 1:2] * self.E2 + L[..., 2:3] * self.N


def normals(h, ds):
    gy, gx = np.gradient(h, ds)           # gy along i (E2), gx along j (E1)
    n = np.stack([-gx, -gy, np.ones_like(h)], -1)
    return n / np.linalg.norm(n, axis=-1, keepdims=True)   # local frame (E1, E2, N)


@njit(parallel=True, cache=True)
def _horizon(h, ds, dirs, radius, out):
    H, W = h.shape
    nd = dirs.shape[0]
    steps = int(radius / ds)
    for i in prange(H):
        for j in range(W):
            acc = 0.0
            for k in range(nd):
                dx, dy = dirs[k, 0], dirs[k, 1]
                best = 0.0
                s = 1
                while s <= steps:
                    x = j + dx * s
                    y = i + dy * s
                    xi, yi = int(x + 0.5), int(y + 0.5)
                    if xi < 0 or yi < 0 or xi >= W or yi >= H:
                        break
                    dist = s * ds
                    t = (h[yi, xi] - h[i, j]) / dist
                    if t > best:
                        best = t
                    s += 1 if s < 8 else 2
                acc += best / np.sqrt(1.0 + best * best)   # sin(horizon elevation)
            out[i, j] = acc / nd


def sky_occlusion(h, ds, radius=2.0, nd=12):
    """Mean sine of the horizon angle over nd azimuths (0 = open sky, ->1 = deep slot)."""
    a = np.arange(nd) * 2 * np.pi / nd
    dirs = np.stack([np.cos(a), np.sin(a)], -1)
    out = np.zeros_like(h, dtype=np.float64)
    _horizon(h.astype(np.float64), ds, dirs, radius, out)
    return out


@njit(parallel=True, cache=True)
def _sun_shadow(h, ds, lx, ly, lz, maxd, out):
    H, W = h.shape
    horiz = np.sqrt(lx * lx + ly * ly)
    if horiz < 1e-6:
        return
    dx, dy = lx / horiz, ly / horiz
    slope = lz / horiz
    steps = int(maxd / ds)
    for i in prange(H):
        for j in range(W):
            s = 1
            while s <= steps:
                x = j + dx * s
                y = i + dy * s
                xi, yi = int(x + 0.5), int(y + 0.5)
                if xi < 0 or yi < 0 or xi >= W or yi >= H:
                    break
                if h[yi, xi] > h[i, j] + slope * s * ds + 0.02:
                    out[i, j] = 1.0
                    break
                s += 1


def sun_shadow(h, ds, L_local, maxd=60.0):
    out = np.zeros_like(h, dtype=np.float64)
    if L_local[2] <= 0:
        out[:] = 1.0
        return out
    _sun_shadow(h.astype(np.float64), ds, float(L_local[0]), float(L_local[1]), float(L_local[2]), maxd, out)
    return out

class Camera:
    """Perspective (fov_deg) or orthographic (ortho_w metres wide) camera."""
    def __init__(self, eye, target, W, H, fov_deg=40.0, ortho_w=None, up=(0, 0, 1)):
        self.eye = np.asarray(eye, float)
        self.W, self.H = W, H
        f = np.asarray(target, float) - self.eye
        self.fwd = f / np.linalg.norm(f)
        r = np.cross(self.fwd, up)
        if np.linalg.norm(r) < 1e-6:
            r = np.cross(self.fwd, (0, 1, 0))
        self.right = r / np.linalg.norm(r)
        self.upv = np.cross(self.right, self.fwd)
        self.fov, self.ortho_w = fov_deg, ortho_w

    def rays(self):
        W, H = self.W, self.H
        xs = (np.arange(W) + 0.5) / W * 2 - 1
        ys = 1 - (np.arange(H) + 0.5) / H * 2
        X, Y = np.meshgrid(xs, ys)
        if self.ortho_w:
            hw = self.ortho_w / 2
            hh = hw * H / W
            eye = self.eye + X[..., None] * hw * self.right + Y[..., None] * hh * self.upv
            return eye.reshape(-1, 3), np.tile(self.fwd, (W * H, 1)), True
        t = np.tan(np.radians(self.fov) / 2)
        d = self.fwd + X[..., None] * t * self.right + Y[..., None] * t * H / W * self.upv
        d /= np.linalg.norm(d, axis=-1, keepdims=True)
        return np.tile(self.eye, (1, 1)), d.reshape(-1, 3), False


def march(h, ds, frame, cam, tmax=5000.0):
    eye, dirs, ortho = cam.rays()
    R = cam.W * cam.H
    hi = np.full(R, -1.0)
    hj = np.zeros(R)
    hd = np.zeros(R)
    _march(h.astype(np.float64), float(ds), frame.O, frame.E1, frame.E2, frame.N,
           np.ascontiguousarray(eye, dtype=np.float64), np.ascontiguousarray(dirs, dtype=np.float64),
           ortho, cam.fwd.astype(np.float64), tmax, hi, hj, hd)
    hit = hi >= 0
    return (hi.reshape(cam.H, cam.W), hj.reshape(cam.H, cam.W), hd.reshape(cam.H, cam.W),
            hit.reshape(cam.H, cam.W))


class GBuf:
    """Per-pixel lookups of texture-space fields at the texel each ray hit."""
    def __init__(self, hi, hj, depth, hit, ds):
        self.hit, self.depth, self.ds = hit, depth, ds
        self.I = np.clip(np.round(hi).astype(int), 0, None)
        self.J = np.clip(np.round(hj).astype(int), 0, None)
        self.fi, self.fj = hi, hj

    def take(self, tex, fill=0):
        H, W = tex.shape[:2]
        I = np.clip(self.I, 0, H - 1)
        J = np.clip(self.J, 0, W - 1)
        out = tex[I, J]
        if out.ndim == 2:
            out = np.where(self.hit, out, fill)
        else:
            out = np.where(self.hit[..., None], out, fill)
        return out


def combine(bufs):
    """Nearest-hit merge of several (GBuf, tag) — e.g. a cliff face plus the beach in front."""
    best = None
    for k, g in enumerate(bufs):
        d = np.where(g.hit, g.depth, np.inf)
        if best is None:
            best, owner = d, np.where(g.hit, k, -1)
        else:
            m = d < best
            best = np.where(m, d, best)
            owner = np.where(m, k, owner)
    return owner, best

"""palette.py — ramps of 8 steps per material: 0-3 shadow family (sky-lit, cooler), 4-7 lit family.

Targets are CIELAB (L*, C*, hue°) for the lit face (step 6) and the shadow face (step 2), measured

Genge's colour rule (p. 35, p. 154): flat base per unit, and *exaggerate* the difference between units
— `unit_ramps(base, n, spread)` makes n sibling ramps whose hue/value differences are pushed apart.

import numpy as np


def lab2rgb(L, a, b):
    fy = (L + 16) / 116
    fx = fy + a / 500
    fz = fy - b / 200
    def finv(t):
        return np.where(t > 6 / 29, t ** 3, 3 * (6 / 29) ** 2 * (t - 4 / 29))
    X, Y, Z = 0.95047 * finv(fx), finv(fy), 1.08883 * finv(fz)
    r = 3.2406 * X - 1.5372 * Y - 0.4986 * Z
    g = -0.9689 * X + 1.8758 * Y + 0.0415 * Z
    bb = 0.0557 * X - 0.2040 * Y + 1.0570 * Z
    rgb = np.stack([r, g, bb], -1)
    rgb = np.where(rgb <= 0.0031308, 12.92 * rgb, 1.055 * np.clip(rgb, 0, None) ** (1 / 2.4) - 0.055)
    return np.clip(rgb, 0, 1)


def lch(L, C, h):
    return np.array([L, C * np.cos(np.radians(h)), C * np.sin(np.radians(h))])

    X = (0.4124 * lin[..., 0] + 0.3576 * lin[..., 1] + 0.1805 * lin[..., 2]) / 0.95047

"""geology.py — rock built from Genge's structural statistics, as heightfields with id fields.

Every builder returns a dict:
  h      heightfield (m) in its frame (face frame: protrusion toward viewer; terrain: elevation)
  ds     grid spacing (m)
  rock   bool mask of rock texels (else ground / background)
  bed    int id of the bed / sheet / flow each texel belongs to (-1 none)
  block  int id of the joint block (unique across the subject) (-1 none)
  unit   int lithological unit (indexes a palette list)
  comp   competence 0..1 per texel (1 = protrudes, ledge-former)
  bg     bool: background (sky) texels for a face; ground texels are rock=False, bg=False
Statistics follow MODEL.md §5: beds vary in thickness vertically and laterally, thin upward where
told to, pinch out; competent beds protrude; joints are few, perpendicular to bedding, and stop
(T-junction) at bed boundaries.
"""
import numpy as np
from scipy import ndimage as ndi


def _rng(seed):
    return np.random.default_rng(seed)


def lognormal(rng, mean, sigma, n):
    mu = np.log(mean) - sigma ** 2 / 2
    return rng.lognormal(mu, sigma, n)


def smooth_noise(n, scale_px, rng, amp=1.0):
    x = rng.standard_normal(n + 4 * int(scale_px) + 8)
    y = ndi.gaussian_filter1d(x, max(scale_px, 0.5))
    y = y[2 * int(scale_px) + 4: 2 * int(scale_px) + 4 + n]
    return amp * y / (y.std() + 1e-9)


def noise2(shape, scale_px, rng):
    a = ndi.gaussian_filter(rng.standard_normal(shape), scale_px)
    return a / (a.std() + 1e-9)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- granite tor (Rough Tor, Fig 2.5/2.9)
def tor(width=12.0, height=7.0, ds=0.05, seed=3, sheet_mean=0.85, sheet_sigma=0.35,
        joint_mean=2.0, joint_sigma=0.45, round_v=0.45, undercut=1.6, round_h=0.25,
        gap=0.10, recess=0.35, taper=0.35, ground_h=0.6):
    """Stacked sheeting 'pillows' cut by vertical joints. round_v: fraction of each sheet's half-height
    that curves back (deep horizontal slots); undercut >1 makes the lower edge curve back more than the
    upper (the overhang Genge shades: 'shadow indicates top of overhang')."""
    rng = _rng(seed)
    W, H = int(width / ds), int((height + ground_h) / ds)
    v = (np.arange(H)[::-1] + 0.5) * ds - ground_h        # up, metres (row 0 = top of image)
    u = (np.arange(W) + 0.5) * ds
    h = np.full((H, W), -4.0)
    bed = -np.ones((H, W), int)
    block = -np.ones((H, W), int)
    # sheets from the ground up
    tops = [0.0]
    while tops[-1] < height:
        tops.append(tops[-1] + float(lognormal(rng, sheet_mean, sheet_sigma, 1)[0]))
    nb = 0
    cx = width / 2 + rng.uniform(-0.1, 0.1) * width
    for k in range(len(tops) - 1):
        v0, v1 = tops[k], min(tops[k + 1], height + 0.4)
        if v0 > height:
            break
        frac = v0 / height
        half = width * (0.5 - taper * frac ** 1.3 * 0.9) + rng.uniform(-0.8, 0.8)
        x0 = max(0.2, cx - half + rng.uniform(-0.7, 0.7) + frac * rng.uniform(-1.5, 1.5))
        x1 = min(width - 0.2, cx + half + rng.uniform(-0.7, 0.7) + frac * rng.uniform(-1.5, 1.5))
        if x1 - x0 < 1.0:
            break
        # joints in this sheet: offset per sheet (T-junctions at sheet boundaries)
        xs = [x0]
        while xs[-1] < x1:
            xs.append(xs[-1] + float(lognormal(rng, joint_mean, joint_sigma, 1)[0]))
        xs[-1] = x1
        if len(xs) > 2 and xs[-1] - xs[-2] < 0.6:
            xs.pop(-2)
        rows = (v >= v0) & (v < v1)
        tv = v1 - v0
        vv = (v[rows] - v0) / tv * 2 - 1                  # -1 bottom .. +1 top
        # pillow profile across height: superellipse, lower edge rounds harder (undercut)
        rv = np.where(vv < 0, round_v * undercut, round_v)
        prof_v = -np.clip(rv * tv * 0.5 * (1 - np.sqrt(np.clip(1 - np.abs(vv) ** 2.2, 0, 1))), 0, None)
        for b in range(len(xs) - 1):
            a0, a1 = xs[b], xs[b + 1]
            cols = (u >= a0) & (u < a1)
            if cols.sum() < 2:
                continue
            tw = a1 - a0
            uu = (u[cols] - a0) / tw * 2 - 1
            prof_h = -round_h * min(tw, 2.5) * 0.5 * (1 - np.sqrt(np.clip(1 - np.abs(uu) ** 3, 0, 1)))
            P = rng.uniform(-recess, recess) + 0.15 * np.sin(k * 1.7)
            tilt = rng.uniform(-0.08, 0.08)
            blockh = P + prof_v[:, None] + prof_h[None, :] + tilt * uu[None, :]
            # joint aperture: a narrow slot at the block's sides
            edge = np.minimum(u[cols] - a0, a1 - u[cols])
            slot = np.clip(1 - edge / max(gap, 1e-3), 0, 1) ** 2 * 0.6
            blockh = blockh - slot[None, :]
            sub = np.ix_(rows, cols)
            h[sub] = blockh
            bed[sub] = k
            block[sub] = nb
            nb += 1
    rock = bed >= 0
    # small surface relief: pitting / weathering hollows (texture, low amplitude)
    h = h + np.where(rock, 0.02 * noise2(h.shape, 2.0, rng), 0)
    # ground strip (grass) in front of the base, with a zigzag top
    gtop = 0.05 + 0.25 * np.abs(smooth_noise(W, 3, rng)) + 0.15 * rng.random(W)
    gmask = v[:, None] < gtop[None, :]
    h = np.where(gmask, 0.8, h)
    rock &= ~gmask
    bg = ~rock & ~gmask
    return dict(h=h, ds=ds, rock=rock, bed=np.where(rock, bed, -1), block=np.where(rock, block, -1),
                unit=np.where(rock, 0, -1), comp=np.where(rock, 1.0, 0.0), bg=bg, ground=gmask,
                u=u, v=v, width=width, height=height + ground_h)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- bedded cliff face (West Bay, Olympic)
def beds_sequence(height, rng, bed_mean=0.6, sigma=0.5, thin_up=0.0, comp_frac=0.35, packets=None):
    """Bed tops from the base up. thin_up: fractional thinning of mean thickness from base to top
    (West Bay: 'cemented layers become more closely spaced and thinner towards the top', p. 98)."""
    tops, comp = [0.0], []
    while tops[-1] < height:
        f = tops[-1] / height
        m = bed_mean * (1 - thin_up * f)
        t = float(lognormal(rng, m, sigma, 1)[0])
        tops.append(tops[-1] + t)
        comp.append(1.0 if rng.random() < comp_frac else 0.0)
    return np.array(tops), np.array(comp)


def bedded_face(width=40.0, height=30.0, ds=0.1, seed=1, bed_mean=0.8, sigma=0.55, thin_up=0.5,
                comp_frac=0.3, protrude=0.35, soft_recess=0.25, dip_deg=0.0, taper=0.35, pinch=0.25,
                joint_mean=3.0, joint_sigma=0.5, joint_keep=0.5, joint_gap=0.08, bluffs=0,
                bluff_setback=4.0, furrows=0.15, skyline_rough=1.0, units=None, base_ground=1.5):
    """A cliff face of beds. Each bed boundary is a wandering line (lateral thickness change: taper);
    some boundaries die out (pinch). Competent beds protrude with a rounded lip and a shadowed soffit.
    Joints perpendicular to bedding, T-terminated at bed boundaries, only joint_keep of the candidate
    joints drawn through more than one bed. bluffs: the face is split into segments set back by
    bluff_setback (Genge §6.4: local horizons)."""
    rng = _rng(seed)
    W, H = int(width / ds), int((height + base_ground) / ds)
    u = (np.arange(W) + 0.5) * ds
    v = (np.arange(H)[::-1] + 0.5) * ds - base_ground
    U, V = np.meshgrid(u, v)
    tops, comp = beds_sequence(height + 2, rng, bed_mean, sigma, thin_up, comp_frac)
    nbnd = len(tops)
    # boundary k wanders laterally (thickness changes along the bed), plus dip
    Vb = np.zeros((nbnd, W))
    dip = np.tan(np.radians(dip_deg))
    for k in range(nbnd):
        wander = smooth_noise(W, 60 / ds * 0.1, rng, 1.0) * taper * (tops[min(k + 1, nbnd - 1)] - tops[max(k - 1, 0)]) * 0.25
        Vb[k] = tops[k] + dip * (u - width / 2) + wander
    Vb = np.maximum.accumulate(Vb, axis=0)              # boundaries never cross
    # bed index per texel
    bed = np.zeros((H, W), int)
    for k in range(1, nbnd):
        bed += (V >= Vb[k][None, :]).astype(int)
    # pinch-outs: some boundaries fade (the two beds merge over a stretch)
    alive = np.ones((nbnd, W), bool)
    for k in range(1, nbnd):
        if rng.random() < pinch:
            c = rng.uniform(0, width)
            L = rng.uniform(0.15, 0.5) * width
            alive[k] = np.abs(u - c) > L / 2 if rng.random() < 0.5 else (u - c) * rng.choice([-1, 1]) < 0
    # profile within each bed: position 0..1 from base to top
    b0 = np.take_along_axis(Vb, np.clip(bed, 0, nbnd - 1), 0)
    b1 = np.take_along_axis(Vb, np.clip(bed + 1, 0, nbnd - 1), 0)
    t = np.clip((V - b0) / np.maximum(b1 - b0, 1e-3), 0, 1)
    cb = comp[np.clip(bed, 0, len(comp) - 1)]
    # competent: protrude with rounded lip and undercut base; soft: recess, weathered into a groove
    lip = np.sin(np.pi * np.clip(t, 0, 1)) ** 0.35
    h_comp = protrude * lip - 0.15 * protrude * (1 - t) ** 6        # undercut near the bed's base
    h_soft = -soft_recess * np.sin(np.pi * t) ** 0.8 * 0.8
    h = np.where(cb > 0.5, h_comp, h_soft)
    # per-bed set back / stand out a little, varying along strike
    setb = np.array([smooth_noise(W, 8 / ds, rng, 0.08) for _ in range(nbnd)])
    h = h + np.take_along_axis(setb, np.clip(bed, 0, nbnd - 1), 0)
    # joints: candidate positions per bed (perpendicular to bedding = vertical here), T-terminated
    block = np.zeros((H, W), int)
    jmask = np.zeros((H, W), bool)
    nb = 0
    through = []   # joints that run through several beds (the few Genge would draw)
    for k in range(nbnd):
        xs = np.cumsum(lognormal(rng, joint_mean, joint_sigma, int(width / joint_mean * 3) + 4)) - rng.uniform(0, joint_mean)
        xs = xs[(xs > 0) & (xs < width)]
        rows = bed == k
        if not rows.any():
            continue
        edges = np.concatenate([[0], xs, [width]])
        idx = np.searchsorted(edges, u) - 1
        block[rows] = (nb + idx[None, :].repeat(H, 0))[rows]
        nb += len(edges)
        for x in xs:
            j = int(x / ds)
            jmask[rows & (np.abs(U - x) < joint_gap * 0.5 + ds * 0.5)] = True
    # open joints are slots
    jd = ndi.distance_transform_edt(~jmask) * ds
    h = h - 0.35 * np.clip(1 - jd / (joint_gap * 1.5 + 1e-3), 0, 1)
    # bluffs: segments set back; a bluff edge makes a local horizon when viewed obliquely
    seg = np.zeros(W, int)
    if bluffs > 0:
        cuts = np.sort(rng.uniform(0.12, 0.88, bluffs)) * width
        seg = np.searchsorted(cuts, u)
        off = np.cumsum(rng.uniform(0.5, 1.0, bluffs + 1) * bluff_setback)
        off -= off[0]
        # rounded bluff noses
        nose = np.zeros(W)
        for c in cuts:
            nose += -1.5 * np.exp(-((u - c) / 1.2) ** 2)
        h = h - off[seg][None, :] + nose[None, :] * 0.0
        h = h + np.where(True, 0, 0)
    # vertical furrows (weathering, p. 98): shallow grooves that break the lit faces
    if furrows > 0:
        fz = smooth_noise(W, 0.6 / ds, rng, 1.0)
        fz = np.clip(fz, 0, None) ** 2
        h = h - furrows * 0.25 * fz[None, :] * (0.6 + 0.4 * noise2((H, W), 6, rng))
    # skyline: top of face rough, stepped over bluffs
    top = height - np.abs(smooth_noise(W, 3 / ds, rng, skyline_rough)) - (seg * rng.uniform(0.2, 1.2))
    bg = V > top[None, :]
    ground = V < 0.02 + 0.3 * np.abs(smooth_noise(W, 2 / ds, rng, 1))[None, :]
    h = np.where(ground, 1.2 + 0.2 * noise2((H, W), 3, rng), h)
    h = np.where(bg, -40.0, h)
    rock = ~bg & ~ground
    unit = np.zeros((H, W), int)
    if units is not None:        # units: list of (from_height, unit_id)
        for z0, uid in units:
            unit[V >= z0] = uid
    return dict(h=h, ds=ds, rock=rock, bed=np.where(rock, bed, -1), block=np.where(rock, block, -1),
                unit=np.where(rock, unit, -1), comp=np.where(rock, cb, 0.0), bg=bg, ground=ground,
                alive=alive, Vb=Vb, u=u, v=v, width=width, height=height + base_ground, seg=seg)

"""genge.py — Genge's drawing rules as a pixel painter.

Input: a screen-space G-buffer dict `g` (see render.gbuffer). Output: an index canvas
(step 0..7 per pixel, -1 for non-rock) plus a colour image through per-unit palettes.

Each rule is a parameter, so any one can be switched off to see what it carries:

  shadows   'photo' = sun + all cast shadows (what a camera records)
            'genge' = no cast shadows at all (Genge §2.9.3: 'should not be included in a sketch')
            'mass'  = cast shadows kept only where they are big masses (>= mass_frac of the rock);
                      small ones dropped, because they obscure structure (the pixel translation)
  ao_inset  step drop in inset areas (Genge's first, grey, layer)            'inset areas shadowed'
  ao_deep   occlusion above which a pixel is forced to the darkest step     'darkest shadow in most inset areas'
  soffit    1-px darkest run under a protruding bed (the 'thick line' on    'shadow indicates top of overhang'
            competent beds with shadowed overhangs, p. 106)
  lip       1-px highlight on the top edge of a protruding lit bed          'highlights and lowlights along the top
                                                                             and bottom of beds' (p. 279)
  bed_lines impersistent 1-step-darker runs on bed boundaries, run length   'discontinuous lines … stroke lengths that
            scaled to the bed's thickness in pixels                          reflect the scale of the objects' (p. 28)
  joints    joint lines, but only the longest, and never more joint pixels  'not obscure the important features by
            than joint_ratio x bedding-line pixels                           adding too many' (p. 61; Fig 4.6)
  contact   darkest 1-px pocket where rock meets the ground in front        'thick line emphasizes foreground'
  skyline   clean silhouette; 1 step darker edge only where rock and sky    'thick line emphasizes horizon'
            are close in value
  streak    texture streaked along the bedding (value noise elongated       Fig 2.8c, Fig 16.7c
            along the bed), amplitude in steps
"""
import numpy as np
from scipy import ndimage as ndi

DEFAULT = dict(shadows='mass', mass_frac=0.06, ao_inset=1.2, ao_lo=0.18, ao_hi=0.45, ao_deep=0.55,
               soffit=True, lip=True, bed_lines=True, bed_run=(0.9, 0.35), joints=True, joint_ratio=0.35,
               joint_min_px=4, contact=True, skyline=True, streak=0.35, streak_len=6.0, seam=0.25,
               up=(0, 1, 0), lit_gamma=0.8, seed=0)

BAYER2 = np.array([[0.0, 0.5], [0.75, 0.25]])


def _bayer(H, W):
    return np.tile(BAYER2, (H // 2 + 1, W // 2 + 1))[:H, :W]


def _hash(*a):
    x = np.uint64(1469598103934665603)
    out = np.zeros(np.broadcast(*a).shape, np.uint64) + x
    for v in a:
        out = (out ^ np.asarray(v).astype(np.uint64)) * np.uint64(1099511628211)
    return (out % np.uint64(1000003)).astype(np.float64) / 1000003.0


def families(g, L, p):
    N = g['N']
    ndl = (N * np.asarray(L)[None, None, :]).sum(-1)
    rock = g['rock']
    self_sh = ndl <= 0.04
    cast = g['shadow'] > 0.5
    if p['shadows'] == 'photo':
        sh = self_sh | cast
    elif p['shadows'] == 'genge':
        sh = self_sh
    else:   # 'mass': keep only big connected cast-shadow masses
        lab, n = ndi.label(cast & rock)
        keep = np.zeros_like(cast)
        if n:
            sizes = ndi.sum(np.ones_like(lab), lab, np.arange(1, n + 1))
            big = np.flatnonzero(sizes >= p['mass_frac'] * max(rock.sum(), 1)) + 1
            keep = np.isin(lab, big)
        sh = self_sh | keep
    lit = rock & ~sh
    return lit, ndl


def value(g, L, p):
    lit, ndl = families(g, L, p)
    up = np.asarray(p['up'], float)
    sky = 0.5 + 0.5 * (g['N'] * up[None, None, :]).sum(-1)
    v_lit = 4.0 + 3.0 * np.clip(ndl, 0, 1) ** p['lit_gamma']
    v_sh = 1.1 + 2.1 * sky
    v = np.where(lit, v_lit, v_sh)
    ao = g['ao']
    # first layer (grey): inset areas lose up to ao_inset steps, staying in their family
    t = np.clip((ao - p['ao_lo']) / max(p['ao_hi'] - p['ao_lo'], 1e-3), 0, 1)
    floor = np.where(lit, 4.0, 0.6)
    v = np.maximum(v - p['ao_inset'] * t, floor)
    return v, lit


def streaks(shape, along, length, seed):
    """Value noise elongated along a direction field (angle radians per pixel, or a constant)."""
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    n = rng.standard_normal(shape)
    if np.isscalar(along):
        ang = np.degrees(along)
        k = np.zeros((1, int(length) * 2 + 1)); k[:] = 1
        n2 = ndi.convolve(n, k / k.sum(), mode='wrap')
        if abs(ang) > 1:
            n2 = ndi.rotate(n2, ang, reshape=False, mode='wrap')
    else:
        n2 = ndi.uniform_filter1d(n, int(length) * 2 + 1, axis=1)
    n2 = ndi.gaussian_filter(n2, 0.5)
    return n2 / (n2.std() + 1e-9)


def boundary(ids, mask, axis):
    """Pixels whose id differs from the neighbour below (axis=0) or to the right (axis=1)."""
    b = np.zeros_like(mask)
    if axis == 0:
        d = (ids[:-1] != ids[1:]) & mask[:-1] & mask[1:]
        b[:-1] |= d
    else:
        d = (ids[:, :-1] != ids[:, 1:]) & mask[:, :-1] & mask[:, 1:]
        b[:, :-1] |= d
    return b


def impersistent(line, run_px, gap_frac, seed):
    """Break a 1-px line mask into runs: walk connected pieces, keep dashes of ~run_px with gaps."""
    lab, n = ndi.label(line, structure=np.ones((3, 3)))
    if n == 0:
        return line
    ys, xs = np.nonzero(line)
    lid = lab[ys, xs]
    order = np.lexsort((ys, xs, lid))
    ys, xs, lid = ys[order], xs[order], lid[order]
    out = np.zeros_like(line)
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    start = 0
    for k in range(1, n + 1):
        end = np.searchsorted(lid, k, side='right')
        m = end - start
        pos = 0
        while pos < m:
            r = max(2, int(rng.lognormal(np.log(max(run_px, 2)), 0.4)))
            gp = max(1, int(r * gap_frac * rng.uniform(0.5, 1.5)))
            sel = slice(start + pos, start + min(pos + r, m))
            out[ys[sel], xs[sel]] = True
            pos += r + gp
        start = end
    return out


def paint(g, L, params=None):
    p = dict(DEFAULT)
    if params:
        p.update(params)
    H, W = g['rock'].shape
    rock = g['rock']
    v, lit = value(g, L, p)
    # texture streaked along the bedding (horizontal here; faces are drawn with bedding ~ horizontal)
    if p['streak'] > 0:
        s = streaks((H, W), p.get('bed_angle', 0.0), p['streak_len'], p['seed'] + 7)
        v = v + p['streak'] * 0.5 * s
        v = np.where(lit, np.clip(v, 4.0, 7.4), np.clip(v, 0.6, 3.49))
    # quantize: flat steps, 2x2 dither only in a narrow seam, never across the family bar
    B = _bayer(H, W)
    frac = v - np.floor(v)
    step = np.floor(v).astype(int)
    seam = np.abs(frac - 0.5) < p['seam']
    step = np.where(seam, np.floor(v - 0.5 + B + 0.0).astype(int), np.round(v).astype(int))
    step = np.where(lit, np.clip(step, 4, 7), np.clip(step, 0, 3))
    ao = g['ao']
    # second layer (black): the most inset areas are the darkest step, whatever the light
    if p['ao_deep'] < 1:
        deep = rock & (ao > p['ao_deep'])
        step = np.where(deep, np.where(ao > p['ao_deep'] + 0.12, 0, 1), step)
    lines = {}
    bed = g['bed']
    comp = g['comp']
    below_bed = np.roll(bed, -1, 0)
    bnd = boundary(bed, rock, 0)                     # pixel above a bed boundary
    bnd_below = np.roll(bnd, 1, 0) & rock           # pixel below it
    # soffit: the pixel under a protruding bed's base = darkest (thick line on competent beds)
    if p['soffit']:
        comp_above = np.roll(comp, 1, 0) > 0.5
        sof = bnd_below & comp_above & (comp < 0.5)
        step = np.where(sof, np.minimum(step, 1 if p['shadows'] != 'photo' else 0), step)
        step = np.where(sof & ~lit, 0, step)
        lines['soffit'] = sof
    # lip: top edge of a protruding bed catches light
    if p['lip']:
        comp_here = comp > 0.5
        lipm = bnd_below & comp_here & lit & (np.roll(comp, 1, 0) < 0.5)
        step = np.where(lipm, np.minimum(step + 1, 7), step)
        lines['lip'] = lipm
    # impersistent bed lines on the remaining boundaries
    bl_px = 0
    if p['bed_lines']:
        other = bnd & ~lines.get('soffit', np.zeros_like(bnd)) & ~np.roll(lines.get('lip', np.zeros_like(bnd)), -1, 0)
        if 'alive' in g:
            other &= g['alive']
        th = g.get('bed_px', None)
        run = p['bed_run'][0] * (np.median(th[rock]) if th is not None and rock.any() else 6.0)
        other = impersistent(other, max(3, run * 2.0), p['bed_run'][1] * 2, p['seed'])
        step = np.where(other, np.maximum(step - 1, np.where(lit, 4, 0)), step)
        lines['bed'] = other
        bl_px = other.sum() + lines.get('soffit', np.zeros(1)).sum()
    # joints: vertical boundaries between blocks inside one bed, longest first, capped
    if p['joints']:
        jb = boundary(g['block'], rock, 1) & ~bnd
        lab, n = ndi.label(jb, structure=np.array([[0, 1, 0], [0, 1, 0], [0, 1, 0]]))
        keep = np.zeros_like(jb)
        if n:
            sizes = ndi.sum(np.ones_like(lab), lab, np.arange(1, n + 1))
            order = np.argsort(-sizes)
            budget = p['joint_ratio'] * max(bl_px, 1) if p['bed_lines'] else np.inf
            used = 0

def run_length_axis0(ids):
    """Per texel: length (texels) of the vertical run of equal ids it sits in."""
    H, W = ids.shape

            from skimage.morphology import skeletonize
            gm = skeletonize(gm)
        except Exception:
            pass

        step = np.where(gm, np.maximum(step - 1, 0), step)
        lines['gully'] = gm

    out = np.array(sky_rgb, np.uint8).copy()
    for k, r in enumerate(ramps):
        m = (unit == k) & hit
        out[m] = r[np.clip(step[m], 0, 7)]
    # haze by depth (value range compressed toward the sky)
    if p['haze'] > 0:
        d = g['depth']
        dn = np.clip((d - np.percentile(d[hit], 5)) / (np.percentile(d[hit], 99) - np.percentile(d[hit], 5) + 1e-6), 0, 1)
        a = (p['haze'] * dn ** 1.3)[..., None]
        skyc = np.asarray(sky_rgb, float)
        hz = out * (1 - a) + skyc * a
        # quantize the haze into 4 bins so it stays palette-like
        out = np.where(hit[..., None], hz, out).astype(np.uint8)
    if p['horizons']:
        d = g['depth']
        up = np.roll(d, 1, 0)
        edge = hit & np.roll(hit, 1, 0) & (up > d * (1 + p['horizon_jump']))

        far = np.roll(edge, -1, 0)
        out = np.where(far[..., None], np.clip(out.astype(int) + 14, 0, 255), out).astype(np.uint8)
        lines['horizon'] = edge
    return out, step, lines

## 1. Master copies by honest downsampling (sheet A0) — the finding that organised everything

I box-downsampled Genge's own Rough Tor drawings (Figs 2.5 and 2.9b) to our three scales, and did
the same to a photo of the tor.

![A0](sheets/A0_mastercopy_downsample.png)

- **Close, 0.1 m/px:** his line drawing survives almost intact. It is a perfectly good pixel
  drawing.
- **Landscape, 0.35 m/px:** the lines turn into grey mush. The photo still reads, because what
  carries it is value masses: the dark crescent under each pillow of rock.
- **Region, 1 m/px:** the drawing is gone. The photo is a lumpy mass with a dark lower left.

**So Genge's line rules are close-range rules. His shading rule, the ambient-occlusion masses,
carries to landscape scale, and only silhouette and big masses carry to region scale.** This is also
why supersampling matters. A 0.1 m joint seen at 0.35 m/px should be a grey hint, not a black line,
so the G-buffer is rendered 3×3 and area-averaged before quantizing (`Scene.gbuffer(ss=3)`).

## 2. Translations, one principle at a time

The method is a heightfield in any frame (a cliff face, a tor, or terrain), built from the
geological statistics in `geology.py`. It is ray-marched to a G-buffer (`engine.py`, `render.py`)
and painted as an index canvas of steps 0–7: 0–3 is the shadow family and 4–7 the lit family, the
studio's convention (`genge.py`, `landscape.py`). Each palette ramp (`palette.py`) is built from
measured L\*C\*h values, so swapping the palette relights the canvas. Every rule below is one
parameter, so it can be switched off and compared.

| # | Genge (page) | pixel translation | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Don't draw cast shadows, only ambient occlusion (p. 37, Fig 2.9) | `shadows='mass'`: keep cast shadows only as big masses (≥ 6% of the rock, or 2% in landscapes) and drop small ones. `ao_inset` takes up to about one step off inset areas without leaving the family (his grey layer). Occlusion above `ao_deep` goes to step 0 or 1 whatever the light (his black layer). | **Carries, strongest result** (sheet B). Back-lit and overcast, the photo-light cliff is a flat brown slab; with the rules, every bed and ledge stays readable. Under raking light, pure Genge (no cast shadows) loses a real value mass, which the critic caught. The pixel version keeps it and shows structure inside it. **Needs rethinking:** a game has sun, so the shadow stays as design and only the small, fragmenting shadows go. |
| 2 | Line width is emphasis: thick at the horizon, the foreground and protruding beds, thin and clean elsewhere (Fig 2.5; p. 106) | There are no lines in pixels, only value edges. The "thick" line becomes a pair: a lit lip on the top edge of a protruding bed (`lip`) and a darkest 1-px soffit under it (`soffit`). The foreground contact becomes a step-0 pocket (`contact`), used at close range only. At 1 m/px it made a black band and was cut. The skyline gets a clean, undithered edge, darkened one step only where rock and sky are close in value. | **Carries at close and landscape range** (Sucia, sheet F, was the critic's best cell). The lip and soffit pair survives downsampling where a dark line alone does not. |
| 3 | Impersistent lines, with stroke length matched to the object's scale (p. 28) | `bed_lines`: bed boundaries broken into dashes. Mean run is 1.8× the bed's thickness in pixels, with gaps. | **Close range only.** At 0.35 m/px and beyond, dashes turn into Morse code and barcode (sheet C, first round). So `line_lod_mpp=0.2` turns drawn lines off above 0.2 m/px, and bedding is then carried only by the lip and soffit pair of beds at least 2 px thick. That is Genge's own "only a proportion of the layers can be drawn" (p. 98). |
| 4 | Don't over-emphasise joints (Fig 4.6; p. 61) | `joints`: longest joint segments first, capped at `joint_ratio` × bedding-line pixels. ## 4. What didn't carry, and why

- **"Don't draw cast shadows" taken literally.** It is right for a notebook and wrong for a game.
  Shadow is our value design and our time of day. What carries is the reason behind it: structure
  has to stay readable inside the shadow.
- **Line hierarchy beyond close range.** Lines are sub-pixel at 0.35 m/px. Genge's own plates
  downsampled prove it (A0).
- **Dashed lines for interpretation (p. 28), and labels, scale bars and looking-towards bearings.**
  These are notebook conventions with no pixel meaning. The nearest in-game equivalent is a debug
  overlay.
- **Vertical exaggeration** is a mistake in sketches but normal in schematic block diagrams (p. 257).
  I kept true scale throughout.

## 5. Dead ends, in order

1. The tor as flat bricks: the pillows had no lens profile and no undercut.
2. Point-sampled rays: every 0.1 m joint became a black line, a tartan grid. Fixed by supersampling.
3. West Bay with every bed competent and every joint open: a brick wall, twice. Then deep furrows
   that read as dark bars.
4. A contact line at 1 m/px: a black band.
5. Canyon terraces taking 85% of each flow's relief: giant stairs. Then rimrock on every flow:
   barcode.
6. Dark bed lines on dark coast rock: hatching.
7. Isolated dither pixels and centre-sample id misses: Morse dots and blue holes. Fixed by
   `despeckle` and the hole fill.

## 6. Rules for other students and the voxsim lanes

1. **Geology before drawing.** Build the rock from its statistics:
   - beds lognormal, thinning upward where the rock does, wandering along strike, some pinching out;
   - thin competent beds in weak ones;
   - joints few, perpendicular to bedding, mostly stopping at bed boundaries.

   Most of what reads as "true" comes from here, not from paint.
2. **Honest pixels.** Supersample, then area-average before quantizing. A feature narrower than a
   pixel is a one-step hint, never a black line.
3. **Structure survives shadow.** Keep cast shadows only as big masses (≥ 2–6% of the object).
   Inside every family, inset areas lose about one step, and the most inset go to the darkest step.
   Check every scene back-lit and overcast: if it goes flat, this rule is missing.
4. **Lines by scale.**
   - At ≤ 0.2 m/px: impersistent bed lines (run ≈ 2× bed thickness), a lip and soffit on protruding
     beds, a few joints (≤ 0.35× bedding pixels), and a contact pocket.
   - Above that: only the lip and soffit on beds at least 2 px thick.
   - At region scale: only silhouette and masses.
5. **On dark rock (lit L\* below ~45), use light lips, not dark lines.**
6. **Landscapes:**
   - horizontal beds make contour-following traces that V up the gullies, so let the geometry draw
     them;
   - draw traces only for the prominent flows;
   - expose rimrock only near crests;
   - separate each local horizon with a lighter, cooler value.
7. **Colour:** a flat base per unit, with units pushed apart (`unit_ramps`), and texture streaked
   along the bedding (`bed_angle`), never stippled.
